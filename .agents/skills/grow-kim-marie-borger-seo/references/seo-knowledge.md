## 1. Evidence hierarchy

Use sources in this order:

1. Current search-platform documentation and policies
2. Current GSC, analytics, inquiry, booking, live HTTP, and rendered HTML
3. Current repository code, content, tests, and repeatable audits
4. Controlled experiments with baseline, one material variable, window, and rollback
5. Independent research with disclosed methods
6. Practitioner interviews, case studies, and SEO-tool estimates
7. Unsupported social claims and folklore

Third-party authority and keyword scores are workflow aids, not Google metrics.

## 2. Durable principles to adopt

### People-first value

- Write for an existing audience and a real booking or learning decision.
- Demonstrate firsthand expertise through process, constraints, examples, media, and outcomes.
- Make authorship and the real person behind the service clear.
- Add substantial original value instead of restating common advice.
- Do not scale content faster than it can receive editorial care.

### Clear page roles

- Give every indexable URL one primary intent and role.
- Put the answer, offer, or decision criteria early.
- Use headings for navigation, not keyword repetition.
- A useful narrow page may be short; word count is not a quality target.
- A large page is not comprehensive merely because it is long.

### Commercial relevance

- Prioritize service and bottom-of-funnel decisions that can produce qualified inquiries.
- Use a small number of guides for questions that genuinely delay or improve a booking decision.
- Measure qualified inquiries, lessons, bookings, and revenue/value, not traffic alone.

### Internal links

- Link every important page from at least one relevant page.
- Use concise, descriptive anchors and meaningful surrounding context.
- Link parent to selected child, child to parent, service to proof, and proof to service/conversion.
- Use sitemaps for discovery support, not as a replacement for contextual links.

### Original proof

- Turn real performances, lessons, reviews, programs, photos, audio, and video into useful proof.
- Preserve facts, consent, attribution, and usage rights across every format.
- Prefer difficult-to-fake authority: real reviews, partners, directories, publications, awards, programs, and inspected work.

### Images and video

- Use sharp, relevant, high-resolution originals while preserving performance.
- Give images descriptive filenames, useful alt text, captions/context, and representative landing pages.
- Use relevant preferred images in visible metadata/schema.
- Add video where the sound, setting, presence, or performance materially improves a booking decision.

### Structured data

- Match structured data to visible content and real entities.
- Keep stable IDs for `Person`, `WebSite`, `WebPage`, `Service`, `Offer`, and related proof.
- Model geographic reach as service coverage, not fake offices.
- Treat schema as clarification and eligibility support, not a ranking promise.

### AI-assisted content

- Use AI for research support, outlines, alternatives, and editing assistance.
- Verify facts and rewrite in Kim's real vocabulary and point of view.
- Add real decisions, examples, media, and constraints.
- Explain substantial automation when users would reasonably care how content was produced.
- Google AI features use the normal Search eligibility and SEO foundations; no special AI schema or AI text file is required.

### Authority and links

- Earn links and mentions through real professional relationships, useful contributions, directories, interviews, and inspectable case studies.
- Use relevant partners whose work Kim would genuinely recommend.
- Qualify paid/sponsored links.
- Track referral and inquiry value, not just link counts.

## 3. Project-specific writing standard

### Voice

- Calm, direct, personal, and concrete
- Professional without press-release language
- Sensitive and restrained for funeral content
- Decision-oriented for weddings, teaching, and events

### Good substance

- What Kim needs to know before confirming
- What changes the musical format, preparation, timing, or price
- When solo viola fits and when another format is better
- What happens before, during, and after the booking
- What the listener, family, couple, student, or organizer experiences
- Real examples and constraints with permission

### Remove on sight

- Statements about "this page"
- "redaktionell" instructions
- "klare Abgrenzung zu"
- Explanations of how headings, examples, FAQs, or keywords were varied
- Phrases written for an SEO operator rather than a customer
- Decorative city facts that do not affect the service decision
- Repeated claims of local planning without real local utility
- Generic claims such as "high quality", "unique", or "professional" without evidence

### Humanization rule

Authenticity comes from firsthand substance and manual editing. It does not come from intentional typos, replacing one punctuation mark, or passing an AI detector.

## 4. Tactics to test

Label these `Experiment`, not fact:

- Clarifying or narrowing a weak page's intent
- Retitling a page with stable impressions but weak CTR
- Answer-first or concise comparison formats
- A small manually rewritten P1 local cohort
- Service-specific proof modules
- Real short video on priority pages
- Image-search optimization for original performance photography
- Case-study amplification through genuine partners and publications
- Contextual internal links from strong service and proof pages
- Consolidating near-synonym topic pages after query mapping
- A URL move only for a documented migration or controlled repositioning with rollback

## 5. Tactics to reject

### Policy and brand risk

- Fake reviews, fake clients, fake locations, fake venue experience, or invented credentials
- Doorway city pages that all funnel to the same generic destination
- Scaled AI pages without original value
- PBNs, tiered link spam, automated link creation, mass exact-match guest-post anchors
- Force-indexing services and click manipulation
- Expired-domain or exact-match satellite networks used to manipulate rankings
- Review manipulation, fake comparison sites, or selectively deceptive competitor reviews
- Hidden text, keyword stuffing, or cloaking

### Unsupported shortcuts

- Treating schema, `sameAs`, unlinked mentions, entity scores, or knowledge panels as direct ranking guarantees
- Republishing the same page on a new URL as a routine indexing fix
- Removing or keeping pages based only on page count
- Removing the brand from every title as a universal rule
- Treating a sitemap, canonical, or indexability check as proof of ranking potential
- Treating AI-detector scores, em dashes, intentional typos, word count, or keyword density as release gates
- Model-version recommendations from dated interviews

## 6. Primary sources

Recheck these because documentation changes:

- [Helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- [Spam policies: doorway and scaled content abuse](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)
- [Crawlable links and anchor text](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable)
- [Build and submit a sitemap](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/build-sitemap)
- [Structured data introduction](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data)
- [Image SEO best practices](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-images)

Current primary-source conclusions:

- Helpful content should provide original value, firsthand expertise, clear authorship, and a satisfying answer.
- Extensive automation and mass production are warning signs when individual pages receive little care.
- Doorway abuse includes substantially similar city/region pages that sit closer to search results than a clear browsable hierarchy.
- Scaled content abuse is about large amounts of unoriginal low-value content, regardless of whether AI created it.
- AI Overviews and AI Mode require normal Search eligibility; there is no special technical or schema requirement.
- Important pages should have contextual internal links.
- High-quality relevant images can improve user appeal and image-result traffic, but must remain fast.

## 7. Transcript provenance

Treat these as practitioner evidence, not search-engine documentation.

### Edward Sturm: "How to Humanize ChatGPT Content"

Source: [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7_bmGoLFfRo), published 2025-05-10.

Adopt:

- Direct, conversational copy
- Short introductions
- Manual editing
- Real examples, images, and video
- Preserving the author's vocabulary

Reject as gates:

- AI detectors
- Em-dash bans
- Intentional typos
- Dated model recommendations

### David Quaid: "The SEO Playbook That Actually Works"

Source: [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9eK5-TpaiPw), published 2025-10-28.

Adopt:

- Contextual links from established pages to narrower relevant pages
- Coherent topic coverage around commercial use cases
- Real business partnerships and useful case studies
- Clarity before cleverness
- Fast answers for scanning users

Test:

- Intent narrowing
- Retitling weak pages
- Answer-first formats

Do not generalize:

- URL republishing
- Removing brands from all titles
- Exact-match satellite domains
- Claimed internal PageRank mechanics

### James Dooley: "What Actually Works in SEO in 2026"

Source: [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EOkrLkKNDrY), published 2026-07-25.

Adopt:

- Bottom-of-funnel services and real decision questions
- Pruning or consolidation only from measured evidence
- Real project/review amplification across useful formats
- Truthful schema and consistent entity data
- Images, video, and qualified-lead measurement

Test:

- Video and project breakdowns on high-value pages
- Image-search cohorts
- Case-study amplification through real partners
- Short commercial pages for narrow fully satisfied intent

Reject:

- PBNs and tiered spam
- Force indexing
- Fake consensus and review manipulation
- Expired-domain abuse
- Multi-domain doorway strategies
- Unverified claims that mentions or schema directly cause rankings
